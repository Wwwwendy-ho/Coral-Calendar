      Addition flashcards

      The 65-fact bank

      Every fact with a first number from 0 to 9 and an answer of 10 or less. Waves follow the school days of the month: days 1–5 are Wave 1, days 6–10 Wave 2, days 11–15 Wave 3, and days 16 onward mix all 65 facts.

      Wave 1: 0, 1, 2 + ___ (30 facts) Wave 2: 3, 4, 5 + ___ (21 facts) Wave 3: 6, 7, 8, 9 + ___ (14 facts)

      The Coral Cove addition decks (“0 + ___” through “9 + ___”, their Say It! versions, and Reef Math Facts) now only include answers of 10 or less. Wrong answer choices above 10 were swapped for numbers 10 or under too.

      ASL

      99 signs, nine at a time

      Alissa's ASL list is split into 11 sets of nine, in the list's own order, starting from the sign she began with (What, on September 25). After sign 99 the list wraps back to sign 1. Each school day uses five signs from the set. Over the set's nine school days, every sign is practiced exactly 5 times, and no sign ever goes more than one day without practice. Signs 1–5 are taught on day 1, and signs 6–9 come in one a day on days 2–5, so each gets a day as the newcomer. A new sign is always practiced again the next day. After nine school days the next set begins. This count runs straight through month ends. Always check a sign against its video before teaching it.

      How we run it

      Supporting Alissa

      Alissa is 12 and autistic, and she's working at about a first-grade level. The plan keeps the work at her level and treats her like the 12-year-old she is.

      Predictable days

      • Same order every day, High Tide or Low Tide. Only the games change.
      • Walk through the Tide Chart together at the start. Tick each block as it finishes so she can see how much is done.
      • Start the timer for each block so she can see how long is left. The bar turns coral with 2 minutes to go. Say that warning out loud too.
      • If a change is coming, such as a new game or a switch to Low Tide, tell her before the block starts.

      Clear, calm communication

      • One direction at a time, in plain words. Show it as well as saying it.
      • Wait 5–10 seconds after a question before helping.
      • Offer two choices instead of open questions: “Crab walk or wall push-ups?”
      • Use First/Then: “First reading, then snack.”

      Body and senses

      • Heavy-work moves (wall push-ups, carrying books, bear crawls) help her settle. Use them before sitting work.
      • Keep music low and use a countdown (“3, 2, 1, freeze”) so nothing stops suddenly. Have headphones ready.
      • Any game can be done in a smaller way: tapping instead of hopping, sitting instead of standing.
      • Competition is always against her own score, never against someone else.

      Kit we use

      • Painter's tape (number line 0–10, hopscotch, answer letters)
      • Index cards and a marker
      • A squishy toy and a soft ball
      • Balloons, and counters or blocks for math
      • Highlighters and colored pencils for worksheets
      • Headphones and a quiet corner
